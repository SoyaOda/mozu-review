What the comparison did not test

  1. A fixed central pivot. Both versions rotate around the same central point. Neither selects the non-holding foot as a support reference or expresses contact-relative body displacement.
  2. A chosen 12° internal difference. Chest and pelvis headings were authored, not measured. Quiet lower-body presentation was assumed before proving that common supported motion was insufficient.
  3. The same upper motion in A and B. Head, shoulders, arms and broom are equal by construction. That comparison cannot show whether changing body transport would improve the action.
  4. Two changes bundled together. A/B toggles both relative yaw and the gray-leg anchoring fade. The existing C pilot isolates relative yaw more closely, but still uses the same pivot.
  5. A stroke that misses its destination. The inherited local arm movement stays too small to reach the opposite foot. A better torso field alone cannot fix the shared hand/tool path.
  6. Engineering checks were narrower than performance. Smoothness, stationary paw vertices and reopening prove that the rig follows its chosen rules. They do not prove that those rules reproduce the source.

Supported turn is plausible. Exclusive foot loading is unknown.

The non-holding foot's sampled outside edge and sole remain fixed in all 120 source frames, with stable results across three mask cutoffs. The holding sole is also fixed in 108 unoccluded samples; the other 12 are excluded. No step or lift is observed.

Both contacts staying planted does not identify which foot bears the load. A pivot-based rotation and a central rotation plus translation can also produce identical geometry. The useful next test is a contact-relative body mechanism, not a claim to have recovered an invisible joint.

Proposed order

Planted contacts → common trunk/pelvis transport → gray-leg and hip accommodation → local arm reach and head attention → optional residual torso twist.

The trunk may move mostly as one unit while both feet stay fixed. The legs must accommodate that relationship; rigidly rotating the whole character including both paws would violate the contact task.

First compare a central common-body case with a non-holding-contact case using the same local arm/head scores. Let the shoulder and tool move with the changed body frame. Only then test a small chest correction. Keep leg accommodation equal when isolating torso twist.
